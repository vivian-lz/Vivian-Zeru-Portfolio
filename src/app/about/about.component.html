I'm Vivian, an Electrical & Computer Engineering student at Vanderbilt University, graduating in May 2027.

I focus on circuit design and analysis, with interests in PCB Design, IC Design, and hardware validation. My studies include microelectronics and graduate coursework in transistor-level analog and digital circuit design.

How it started

A maker before I had the word for it.

I have always loved math, creative problem-solving, and making things. At eleven, videos of young inventors turning everyday materials into useful devices made me want to learn how to build electronics that could help people.

Before engineering, I drew, painted, practiced calligraphy, and made things out of duct tape and craft supplies. That creativity is still part of how I approach a circuit or a new design problem: patiently work through it until I understand why it works.

What keeps me interested

From a circuit to something real.

My eight-month hardware internship at Apple gave me experience in power electronics, analog and digital design, PCB design, validation, measurement equipment, scripting, cross-functional collaboration, and board bring-up.

I enjoy the connection between design and evidence—understanding how a circuit should behave, then investigating what it actually does.

People & perspective

More perspectives make better engineering.

As a Black woman in engineering, I hope to encourage others to pursue STEM and bring their own experiences to the field. My involvement in Women in Science and Engineering during high school helped shape that motivation.

Through mentoring, ECE Tech Crew, and Engineers Without Borders, I value helping other students build confidence and making engineering useful to the communities around us.
